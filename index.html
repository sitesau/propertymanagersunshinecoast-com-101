Property Managers Sunshine Coast Beginner Guide Explained

Appointing a manager for the first time comes with a new vocabulary: management fees, letting fees, routine inspections, bond lodgement, condition reports. Reading a plain overview of what to expect from Sunshine Coast property managers before signing anything gives you a baseline for the conversations ahead.

Start with the service scope. Tenant screening, leasing, routine inspections, landlord reporting and rental appraisals form the core of most management offers. Ask which items are included in the ordinary management fee and which attract a separate charge, because advertising, renewals, tribunal appearances and extra inspections can all change the real cost.

Then test local fit. A manager handling a beachside unit in Mooloolaba faces different tenant demand, maintenance access and inspection logistics than one managing a family home in Buderim or a hinterland rental in Nambour.

Reading the Fee Schedule Past the Headline Rate

A single percentage figure can look attractive while hiding most of the cost. Before appointing anyone, request a written schedule covering:

  • Management fee: the ongoing percentage or fixed amount charged while the tenancy runs.
  • Letting fee: the charge for finding and placing a tenant at the start or after a vacancy.
  • Inspection charges: whether routine inspections are included or billed separately.
  • Renewal and advertising costs: fees for lease renewals, marketing and listing preparation.
  • Additional services: tribunal appearances, extra inspections or out of hours work.

Comparing two or three schedules side by side usually reveals more than any single quote. A manager with a slightly higher headline rate but fewer add on charges can end up cheaper across a full lease term.

Queensland Compliance Checks Beginners Miss

Some management tasks sit inside Queensland tenancy rules, so confirm who holds the appropriate licence or registration for regulated real estate work, who supervises the work and how compliance is checked.

Bond handling is the most direct test. General tenancy bonds in Queensland are capped at 4 weeks rent and must be lodged with the Residential Tenancies Authority within 10 days of receipt. Ask how lodgement evidence is recorded and passed to you.

Rent increases follow their own process. At least 12 months must generally pass between increases for general tenancies. Periodic tenancy increases require at least 2 months written notice, while fixed term increases carry additional agreement and notice requirements. Exemptions can apply, so ask how the manager checks the current RTA position before issuing any notice.

Matching Your Questions to the Property

Local knowledge should produce specific answers rather than general promises. The table below shows how the right questions shift with the property.

For Mooloolaba, ask how comparable lettings are chosen and how long term residential management is separated from short stay assumptions. For Buderim, ask how tenant screening weighs household fit and maintenance expectations for a family home. For Nambour, ask for recent leasing experience with similar stock and how maintenance coordination lines up trades, tenant availability and owner approval.

Documents and Reporting to Collect First

A good proposal leaves you with papers you can compare side by side. Ask for sample statements and reporting before appointment so rent entries, invoices, inspection outcomes and repair notes can be assessed in advance.

For inspections, confirm how often routine inspections are scheduled, what photo evidence is included and how unresolved maintenance is carried forward between visits. Scheduled property condition reports with photos, tenant communication and maintenance follow up are the items to look for.

For leasing, ask who reviews applications, how references are handled and when you approve a preferred applicant. Lease agreements should comply with Queensland law, and the first statement should arrive on a predictable cycle. End of year documentation matters as well, particularly for owners building a portfolio.

  1. Collect written fee schedules. Request the full schedule for management, letting, inspection, renewal and additional fees from each manager under consideration.
  2. Verify compliance basics. Confirm the appropriate Queensland licence or registration, the bond lodgement process and how rent increase rules are checked.
  3. Request sample reporting. Ask to see sample statements, inspection photos and condition reports before signing a new authority.
  4. Match questions to the property. Frame your questions around your suburb, property condition, likely tenant pool and service needs, then appoint.
